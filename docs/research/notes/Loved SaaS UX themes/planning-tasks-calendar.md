# Planning, tasks, and calendar UX

Research date: 13 October 2026. Scope is commercial task, weekly-planning, and calendar products. Email clients, note apps, and design tools were not deep-read. Ratings, prices, and user counts appear only when a fetched page stated them. Where two pages disagree, both are listed. This file does not score products for Kairos and does not write cross-product themes.

## Which commercial products both make money and have a repeated reputation for an interface people like?

### Takeaway

The products with a repeated, specific reputation for an interface people like using — and a visible way they charge — are Things 3, Todoist, Fantastical, Sunsama, Structured, Akiflow, TickTick, and, for a narrower power-user audience, OmniFocus 4. Notion Calendar (formerly Cron) and Morgen are praised for craft on a thin independent review base. Reclaim.ai makes money as a calendar layer and scores high on G2, but reviewers are rating scheduling behavior more than a distinctive screen. Motion makes money and reviewers praise automatic placement of tasks; the same reviews keep calling the interface cluttered. Clockwise, a well-liked focus-time product, shut down on 27 March 2026. Height shut down on 24 September 2025 and was a project tool, not a daily planner.

### Inferences

- “People like the interface” and “people like that it schedules for them” are different reputations. Things, Fantastical, Structured, and Todoist are in the first group. Motion and Reclaim are in the second. Sunsama and Akiflow are in both, with the price as the steady objection.
- A high G2 star rating with a double-digit review count (Fantastical 16, Morgen 29, Vimcal 1, Cron 5) is not the same kind of evidence as Todoist’s 800-plus or Structured’s 167K App Store ratings.
- The category lost two liked calendar products to talent acquisitions that did not keep the software (Clockwise in 2026, Mayday after 2024). That is a commercial fact about this market, separate from any interface lesson.

### Gaps

- Revenue and active-user counts for Things, Todoist, Fantastical, Sunsama, Akiflow, TickTick, OmniFocus, and Morgen were not found on a primary page in this pass. Reclaim’s 320,000 users and Clockwise’s hour counts are vendor claims at a moment in time, cited above.
- Todoist’s current price was not read on todoist.com. OmniFocus’s current store page was not opened. Motion’s Individuals versus Teams price did not render cleanly.
- Apple Reminders was not reviewed. It appears only as a backend Structured Pro and Fantastical can read.

## For a deep shortlist of 10–12 products, what is the product for, what interaction do people praise, and how is that interaction built?

### Takeaway

The praised interactions are concrete and different from each other: Things is a paper-like list with a draggable plus button and type-to-navigate; Todoist is one typed line that becomes a dated task; Fantastical is one typed sentence that becomes an event, usually from the menu bar; Sunsama is a keyed daily ritual that refuses an overfull day; Structured is a single vertical timeline; Akiflow is a system-wide command bar plus time-slot containers; TickTick is many views in one cheap app; OmniFocus is a Review perspective that shows one project; Notion Calendar and Morgen are keyboard calendars; Reclaim and Motion write blocks onto a calendar, one as a layer and one as a replacement, and only Motion moves those blocks again after you look away.

### Cited Findings

#### Things 3 (Cultured Code) — personal to-dos on Apple devices

- Job: a solo to-do list. No sharing, no web or Windows or Android client. Areas hold projects; projects hold to-dos and headings. — [Gupta, 1 Apr 2026](https://guptadeepak.com/tools/top-10-task-management-apps-2026/); [features page](https://culturedcode.com/things/features/)
- Today list: after a morning plan, Today is the daily screen. Calendar events the user chose sit grouped at the top; to-dos sit below. This Evening is a separate section on that same day for items that wait until home. Upcoming is the week-ahead list (scheduled to-dos, repeating to-dos, deadlines, calendar events), and reschedule is drag and drop. — [features page](https://culturedcode.com/things/features/)
- Magic Plus: tap creates a to-do. Drag the button to insert at a row in Today, under a heading, or on a day in Upcoming. Inside a project, drag it to the left margin to create a heading. Drop it on the Inbox target that appears at the left to capture without leaving the current list. On iOS, a swipe on a row both reveals actions and enters multi-select; dragging a finger across the circles selects a range. — [features page](https://culturedcode.com/things/features/)
- Quick Find / Type Travel: on the Mac, typing with no shortcut opens search. The first keystroke shows results. If the typed string is a tag, it becomes an app-wide tag filter. Slim Mode hides the sidebar with a two-finger swipe; the window title still switches lists. — [features page](https://culturedcode.com/things/features/)
- When-to-do-it, not a calendar grid: hover a row and a calendar button appears (Jump Start). Choices are Today, This Evening, a reminder time, a later date, or Someday. Natural language in that popover completes fragments: “Tom” to tomorrow, “Sat” to Saturday, “Wed 8pm” sets a reminder. There is no native time-block grid; reviewers still cite that absence. Calendar events are read-only context at the top of Today. — [features page](https://culturedcode.com/things/features/); [Gupta, 1 Apr 2026](https://guptadeepak.com/tools/top-10-task-management-apps-2026/)
- Open a to-do and it animates into a white sheet. Tags, checklist, start date, and deadline stay in the corner until used. Checklists can be pasted from a bulleted list. Headings can be dragged to move every to-do under them, and archived with those to-dos. Progress pies sit on projects. The sidebar groups projects under areas. — [features page](https://culturedcode.com/things/features/)
- Empty-state and motion intent, from the company: they prototype motion in After Effects, keep an effect only if it still explains the action after repeated use, and removed the bottom button row so the list runs to the bottom of the phone like a sheet of paper. That interview is from the 2017 Things 3 launch; the features page still describes the same motion toolkit. — [SSPAI interview, 2017, older](https://sspai.com/post/39724); [features page](https://culturedcode.com/things/features/); [iPhone X post, Nov 2017, older](https://culturedcode.com/things/blog/2017/11/hello-x/)
- September 2026 addition, on top of that UI: Siri AI can create a to-do and then, in the same conversation, add notes, start dates, and alerts without opening the app. Spotlight indexes the database; the user can turn that off. — [MacRumors, 14 Sep 2026](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/14/things-3-updated-for-ios-27/); [Cultured Code, 14 Sep 2026](https://culturedcode.com/things/blog/2026/09/things-for-os-27-and-siri-ai/)
- A 2026 roundup’s praised points match the feature page: Today, areas, no nagging, no gamification, one-time purchase. The same roundup says natural language is a Things weakness relative to Todoist. Cultured Code’s own Jump Start copy says natural language exists; the gap reviewers mean is sentence-style capture of the Fantastical or Todoist Quick Add kind, not the absence of any date parser. — [The Tool Chief](https://thetoolchief.com/best-of/best-task-management-apps/); [features page](https://culturedcode.com/things/features/)

#### Todoist (Doist) — cross-platform capture into lists

- Job: a task inbox that stays a list. PCMag: the default is the list; calendar and kanban are optional views, not the home screen. Projects nest. Filters are a query language. — [PCMag](https://au.pcmag.com/productivity/84696/todoist)
- Quick Add: press `Q`, or click Add task. On desktop, a global Quick Add shortcut works while another app is focused; the help page says that global shortcut is desktop-only, and points phone users at the widget. As the user types, recognized dates highlight. A red Dynamic Add button on mobile can be dragged to a row so the new task lands there, which is the same insert gesture as Things’ Magic Plus, documented in help pages updated 1 September and 30 September 2026. — [Quick Add help, updated 1 Sep 2026](https://www.todoist.com/help/todoist/features/use-task-quick-add-in-todoist-va4Lhpzz); [tasks help, updated 30 Sep 2026](https://www.todoist.com/help/todoist/features/introduction-to-tasks-080OAXric)
- What the parser accepts, from the official date article: `tomorrow`, `tom`, `next week` (next Monday unless Settings change it), `next month`, `mid January` (the 15th), `end of month`, `in 5 days`, `in 2 hours`, `in the morning` (9am), `in the afternoon` (12pm), `in the evening` (7pm), `someday` (two months out), `every other Tuesday starting March 3`. A deadline is a separate field typed in braces, for example `{march 30}`. In Today or Upcoming, a new task defaults to today; `no date` leaves it undated. `#Work` sets the project. The project chip in Quick Add cannot be hidden. Settings let the user show or hide priority, date, deadline, assignee, reminders, labels, location, and attachment, reorder those chips, and keep the description field open. Those settings sync across platforms. — [dates help](https://www.todoist.com/help/todoist/features/introduction-to-dates-and-time-q7VobO); [customize Quick Add, updated 16 Sep 2026](https://www.todoist.com/help/todoist/features/customize-quick-add-in-todoist-eqRRlZJNN)
- An April 2026 walkthrough of the same gesture: `Review Q2 metrics report tomorrow at 2pm p1 #Work` becomes one task. The reviewer’s complaint is the next step the product does not take: P1–P4 is the whole priority scale, and there is no workload view, so the capture line does not decide the day. — [Gupta, 1 Apr 2026](https://guptadeepak.com/tools/top-10-task-management-apps-2026/)
- Efficient App (2026) describes a voice feature called Ramble: speak a dump, and the app splits it into tasks. That behavior was not confirmed in the official help pages fetched here. — [Efficient App](https://efficient.app/best/todo-list)
- PCMag’s interface point is negative space: fewer buttons than Asana, and AI that stays out of the way. Wirecutter’s point is the same: minimal surface, many functions underneath, including an AI assistant the reviewer found useful rather than nagging. — [PCMag](https://au.pcmag.com/productivity/84696/todoist); [Wirecutter](https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/reviews/best-to-do-list-app/)

#### Motion — constraint solver that owns the task list

- Included because the mechanism is the clearest in the category, not because reviews praise the chrome. See the dislike section.
- A task is not placed unless it has a duration and a deadline or a priority. No duration: it cannot be scheduled. No deadline and no priority: it will not be placed. A future start date waits, even if today is empty. Connected-calendar events marked busy always win; Motion moves the task. Personal events on a calendar that is not “My Calendar,” or events not marked busy, are ignored. Long tasks split into chunks with a minimum size. If the constraints cannot be met, the state is “Could not fit” and the task stays off the calendar until duration, deadline, or priority changes. — [Motion help](https://www.usemotion.com/help/time-management/auto-scheduling/reference-auto-scheduling/how-auto-scheduling-works-behind-the-scenes)
- Precedence, from the same help center: ASAP first, then hard deadlines (Motion will schedule outside working hours to hit a hard deadline), then sooner due dates, then priority, then duration. Recurring tasks beat one-off tasks in WorkifyLab’s reading of the docs. Every task shows an ETA. If a higher-priority task or a new meeting appears, lower tasks move again; the help page lists “tasks keep moving” as expected behavior, and the fix is to change priority or deadline, or lock the task. — [Motion help, auto-scheduling overview](https://www.usemotion.com/help/time-management/auto-scheduling); [behind the scenes](https://www.usemotion.com/help/time-management/auto-scheduling/reference-auto-scheduling/how-auto-scheduling-works-behind-the-scenes); [WorkifyLab](https://workifylab.com/best-calendar-apps-2026/)
- There is no native Todoist, Asana, Linear, Notion, or Jira integration. The integrations page, via WorkifyLab, lists Google Calendar, Outlook 365, iCloud, Zoom, Teams, Meet, Gmail, Siri, Zapier, and a public API. Moving in is the product. An email to tasks@usemotion.com becomes a task, per a 2026 pricing write-up. — [WorkifyLab](https://workifylab.com/best-calendar-apps-2026/); [Catch](https://www.catchagent.ai/compare/motion-pricing)
- WorkifyLab’s classification, which matches the docs: Motion and Reclaim are constraint solvers that rewrite the calendar; Morgen rewrites only while planning; Amie, Akiflow’s assistant, Motion’s chat, and Calendly’s Callie are language features bolted on, and several vendors do not document what those features do. — [WorkifyLab](https://workifylab.com/best-calendar-apps-2026/)

### Inferences

- The interactions with the longest praise are the ones that finish in one gesture and show the parse before commit: Things Magic Plus and Type Travel, Todoist Quick Add, Fantastical’s menu-bar sentence, Akiflow’s command bar.
- The planning products people describe as calm add a second step the capture apps omit: a forced pass over “what will not fit” (Sunsama’s workload warning and `D` to defer; Structured’s timeline, which makes the overflow visible as overlapping blocks; Microsoft To Do’s My Day, noted in the screening list, which starts empty).
- Auto-schedule is three mechanisms. Collapsing them into “AI calendar” hides the control difference: Motion moves blocks after the fact, Reclaim books blocks from another tool, Morgen stops when the user accepts the plan.

### Gaps

- Default keyboard shortcuts for Akiflow’s command bar were not on the official page fetched here. Option+Space is one reviewer’s binding.
- Fantastical’s current free-versus-Premium feature list is unpublished. The 2020 MacStories split should not be treated as the 2026 paywall.
- No onboarding recording or empty-state screenshot was captured for any product. Empty-state behavior is documented only where a manual states it (Microsoft To Do My Day; Structured’s Rise and Shine / Wind Down; Sunsama’s shutdown screen).
- Vimcal is omitted from the twelve because its independent review base is one G2 review. The mechanics that are documented (five time zones on the EA plan, holds that delete themselves, $25 a month annually or $35 monthly, EA at $68.75 annually) are in the screening list.

## What do reviewers still dislike?

### Takeaway

The repeated dislikes are price, a paywall in front of a basic planning view, setup time, a mobile app that is only for capture, AI that either nags or rewrites the day without a lock, and feature density. They are not evenly distributed. Things is disliked for what it refuses to add. Motion is disliked for clutter, billing, and a phone app the company calls desktop-first. Sunsama, Akiflow, and Fantastical are disliked for the bill more than for the screen.

### Inferences

- Price complaints stick when the product is a layer (Sunsama, Akiflow, Reclaim, Morgen) because the user is already paying for the system of record. Things’ one-time fee draws the opposite complaint: the fee is per device, but it ends.
- “Clutter” in these reviews usually means either too many controls on the home screen (Motion, ClickUp, Asana) or a list that never helps pick today’s three (Todoist). Structured and Sunsama are the products whose whole screen is the answer to that second complaint, and they are also the ones called expensive or shallow as project tools.

### Gaps

- Reddit threads were not fetched. WorkifyLab says its tooling could not reach Reddit; this pass did not add a Reddit sample. Complaint patterns above are from G2, Capterra, Trustpilot as reported by secondary articles, App Store listings, and named reviews.
- No systematic coding of review text was done. “Top complaint category” figures are only as good as WorkifyLab’s tally (Akiflow missing-features 18/99; Motion billing on Trustpilot).
