# Loved tools show one next action

The commercial products people describe as pleasant to use in October 2026 open on **one next action**, keep everything else in a quiet list, turn a typed line into a dated item only after the date is visible, and wait for a yes before an assistant files, archives, or reschedules. That is a screen rule, not a visual style. A market screen of **344** paid products, built on 13 October 2026, found a sourced price or revenue figure *and* a 2025–2026 essay that praises the interface for only **eight** products: Figma, Notion, Linear, Superhuman, Slack, Loom, Todoist, and Pitch. The binding constraint was interface evidence. Hundreds of products clear “makes money,” and stretching that eight to forty would mean treating a G2 satisfaction rank, or fame, as praise of a screen. The working set below is the **59** products the five category notes actually opened down to a gesture. Fifteen mechanics repeat. Six fit Kairos’s Notebook page as they stand, five are good ideas wearing a SaaS costume this product should not copy, and four conflict with a local student companion that reads Canvas and does not write back. Five hundred interfaces were not reviewed by hand.

## The strict list is eight and the working list is fifty-nine

The question was what top-performing commercial SaaS and paid software are doing in the interface as of **13 October 2026**, especially products people review highly and like to use, and which of those patterns, condensed to ten or fifteen themes, belong in Kairos. Kairos is a local-first companion that reads a student’s Canvas, then helps them plan and study. It does not submit, comment, or post. The Notebook system, adopted 7 October 2026, is the scoring rule: a near-white page, hairline rows, one subject per screen, exactly one highlighter, sentence case, and no glass, gradients, glow, mascots, card grids, or losable streaks ([Kairos Notebook](../../design-system/kairos/MASTER.md)).

The sample has two layers, and they answer different questions. The market screen is breadth. It used five public list types that were actually fetched: G2’s 2026 Best Software Awards, announced 18 February 2026, covering 46 lists and placing less than 1% of over 175,000 vendors; a weekday database of 153 US-listed SaaS companies whose last reported quarter was annualized by multiplying by four; Sacra pages that label their figures as estimates; GetLatka’s public top-private ranking; and a 5 September 2026 count of the Setapp catalog at **396** apps, against marketing copy that still said 240+, 270+, or 330+ ([G2](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/g2-announces-2026-best-software-award-winners-302691607.html); [PublicSaaSCompanies.com](https://publicsaascompanies.com/); [Sacra on Notion](https://sacra.com/research/notion-at-865m-year-growing-82-yoy/); [Latka](https://getlatka.com/latka-top); [TechLila](https://www.techlila.com/setapp-app-list/)). Operator posts add a price someone pays, not company revenue. Capterra 2025–2026 shortlists, a full Latka revenue table, Microsoft and Block earnings, and the YC and a16z portfolios were not retrieved. Annualized-quarter figures are not GAAP, and Sacra and Latka numbers are estimates or database rows, not filings.

Each of the 344 names sat on a page fetched for that screen. The UI flag is `praised`, `mixed`, `disliked`, or `unknown`. **Unknown means no 2024–2026 interface source was fetched.** It does not mean the interface is bad. G2 satisfaction was treated as a commercial signal, not as interface praise, except where a separate essay or complaint thread talked about the screen. **320** rows are unknown, **17** are praised, and **7** are disliked. The praised set in the essays actually fetched is a small canon: Linear, Notion, Superhuman, Figma, Slack, Todoist, Raycast, Loom, Pitch, and a second cluster (Stripe, Things, Arc, Warp, Vercel, Framer, Flighty, Halide) whose revenue was often missing ([merge.rocks](https://merge.rocks/blog/ux-best-practices-weve-learned-from-productivity-apps-like-notion); [Blake Crosley](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/design-studies-collection); [Dania Syrofi](https://daniasyrofi.com/writing/details-that-make-interfaces-feel-better/)). The strict shortlist keeps only the eight that also have a sourced price or revenue figure. Loom and Pitch are in that eight and were **not** deep-read: the operator pass found no usable page for either and left them off its table rather than scoring them from memory. Raycast, Stripe, Things, Arc, Warp, Vercel, Framer, Flighty, and Halide are the near-misses the screen itself named.

The working shortlist is the deep reads, not that eight. A product is on it when a category note, fetched in this same pass, states what the product is for, names an interaction people praise or a mechanic the vendor documents, says how that interaction is built, and records what reviewers still dislike or says that dislike is unknown. Products that appear only as a screening-table name, with the interface marked not assessed, stay in the addendum and out of the shortlist. That rule produces **59** products: 12 inbox, 12 planning, 10 notes, 12 operator tools, and 13 consumer. The five slices were read separately and did not score Kairos. This report is the first place those notes are scored against the Notebook rules.

The honest limit is the size of the claim. **Five hundred interfaces were not opened one by one.** Most of the 344 were never given an interface source. Review counts disagree by site and by month, and where two pages conflict both numbers are kept. Revenue inside a design essay (Linear at $100 million in June 2025, Superhuman at roughly $35 million ARR at the July 2025 acquisition) stays a secondary claim. No source in the notes scored these products for a student Canvas app. That scoring is new here, and it is a design judgment against MASTER.md, not a new user study.

## Three hundred twenty products have no interface source

The commercial universe is wide, and “large and paid” does not mean people like the screen. Salesforce can be G2’s top 2026 company, with an annualized **$45.36 billion** on a July 2026 quarter, and still draw a 2026 practitioner thread about related lists that load separately, an old report builder, setup search, and list views that lose your place ([PublicSaaSCompanies.com](https://publicsaascompanies.com/); [r/salesforce](https://www.reddit.com/r/salesforce/comments/1rbe92f/what_uiux_part_of_salesforce_irritates_you_the/)). Jira is rank **29** on G2’s 2026 highest-satisfaction list and is also the subject of 2025 Atlassian community threads calling the new navigation a regression: buried functions, more clicks, a heavier page, slower boards ([G2 satisfaction](https://www.g2.com/best-software-companies/highest-satisfaction); [Jira UX thread](https://community.atlassian.com/forums/Jira-questions/Does-anyone-else-feel-that-these-10-UX-changes-in-Jira/qaq-p/3080266)). Workday’s 2026R2 changes drew complaints that related actions hide until hover and that time entry is buried ([r/workday](https://www.reddit.com/r/workday/comments/1w12agk/feedback_on_ui_changes_in_2026r2/)). A provisioning ranking, which is admin pain rather than a visual study, scores SAP S/4HANA 10/10, Workday 9.5, Oracle ERP Cloud 8.8, Salesforce 8.5, and Jira 7.5 ([Stitchflow](https://www.stitchflow.com/blog/enterprise-apps-ranked-by-provisioning-difficulty)). The disliked rows stay in the universe. The seven names are Jira, Oracle ERP Cloud, Salesforce, Salesforce Sales Cloud, Salesforce Service Cloud, SAP S/4HANA, and Workday. ServiceNow, ADP, and UKG had no 2025–2026 “unpleasant” source in the screen, so they stay unknown. Reddit threads are enough to flag a dislike. They are not a measure of how widely the dislike is held.

Setapp contributes a named slice of notes, planning, design, and developer apps, not the full 396. Inclusion means the app is in a paid bundle. It does not mean buyers praise that app’s interface. The table keeps the name, the screen’s category, and the UI flag. Revenue figures stay the ones already cited above, and they are annualized quarters or estimates, not GAAP.

The table is every name in that screen. Category is the screen’s bucket. The UI flag is only from a design essay, an operator calling the interface fast or calm, or a 2025–2026 complaint thread. **320 unknown, 17 praised, 7 disliked.** Praised: Arc, Figma, Flighty, Framer, Halide, Linear, Loom, Notion, Pitch, Raycast, Slack, Stripe, Superhuman, Things, Todoist, Vercel, Warp. Disliked: Jira, Oracle ERP Cloud, Salesforce, Salesforce Sales Cloud, Salesforce Service Cloud, SAP S/4HANA, Workday. A revenue line is not evidence that people like the interface.

## Fifteen mechanics repeat on the screens people praise

The mechanics below are the screen, the default, or the gesture. A slogan that showed up in marketing and never landed on a control is left out. Each one is scored in the next section.

### The day opens on one thing

The home screen is one object, and the rest is a list. Things puts Today under the events you chose to show. Todoist’s default is the list, with calendar and kanban as optional views. OmniFocus Review shows one project. Mercury’s teardown puts cash balance first and leaves pay and recent activity as the next actions. Google Maps opens on the map, then a place card whose button is Directions. Duolingo’s path and a Headspace course both answer “what is next” with a single node. Sunsama’s planning screen is the day, not a workspace of every imported tool ([Cultured Code](https://culturedcode.com/things/features/); [PCMag on Todoist](https://au.pcmag.com/productivity/84696/todoist); [OmniFocus manual](https://support.omnigroup.com/documentation/omnifocus/universal/4.3.3/en/perspectives/); [PCMag UK](https://uk.pcmag.com/gps-navigation/131286/google-maps)).

### What does not fit gets deferred on purpose

Capture apps stop at the typed line. The planning apps people call calm add a second step: a pass over what will not fit. Sunsama sums planned time against a shutdown time and binds defer to D and backlog to Z. Structured’s timeline makes overflow visible as blocks, and Replan lists leftovers so they can be rescheduled or dumped. HEY’s Focus & Reply hides the rest of the Imbox and walks only the Reply Later pile. Microsoft To Do’s My Day, in the planning screen’s extra list, starts empty each morning. Motion is the opposite mechanism: the software keeps placing tasks after you look away ([Sunsama help](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/usage-guides/daily-planning/); [Structured](https://help.structured.app/en/categories/1823490); [HEY](https://www.hey.com/features/)).

### A typed line shows its date before it saves

The interactions with the longest praise finish in one gesture and show the parse before commit. Todoist highlights the date as you type and accepts `tomorrow` and `{march 30}` as different fields. Fantastical’s menu-bar sentence keeps “Coffee with Rebecka” as the title and files the place and the reminder. Akiflow’s command bar takes “dentist next Thursday at 2.” Things’ Jump Start completes “Wed 8pm” inside a popover. Apple Reminders, from iOS 26, parses “tomorrow at 3pm” in a panel that does not open the app ([Todoist dates](https://www.todoist.com/help/todoist/features/introduction-to-dates-and-time-q7VobO); [Anders Norén](https://andersnoren.se/gear/software/fantastical-review/); [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/create-new-reminder-single-tap-ios/)).

### The shortcut sits beside the action that runs it

Superhuman’s command palette is how you learn the keyboard: type the action, it runs, and the dedicated shortcut is shown beside it. Linear’s Cmd-K lists actions for the current view and applies the chosen action to the selection. Figma’s actions menu filters on a word such as “align,” and Shift-A is the layout gesture you repeat all day. Raycast’s default hotkey is Option-Space. Vercel’s Cmd-K opens on recent items when the query is empty. Notion Calendar kept Cron’s palette. Arc uses Cmd-T for the same job because T was already new-tab. Stripe uses search plus `?` rather than a documented Cmd-K ([Superhuman getting started](https://email-tools.me/posts/how-to-use-superhuman/); [Linear search](https://linear.app/docs/search); [Vercel Geist](https://vercel.com/geist/command-menu); [Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/dashboard/basics)).

### Incoming work sits in piles a person can correct

Unread is the wrong counter in the clients people praise. Superhuman counts every conversation in a split. HEY separates new from previously seen and asks, once, where a sender lives. Shortwave wants a star or a todo instead of a bold subject left in place. Spark’s listing uses Done and Set Aside. Gmail, Outlook, and Apple Mail classify first. The person can drag a Gmail message to another tab on the desktop, train Outlook with “always move,” or Control-click a sender in Apple Mail. The failure mode is the same in both designs: important mail hidden in Paper Trail, Other, or Promotions, and low-value mail left in the primary pile ([Superhuman](https://help.superhuman.com/hc/en-us/articles/46005793275277-Structure-Your-Inbox); [nerdontour](https://nerdontour.net/hey-com-long-term-review-its-inbox-by-gmail-on-steroids/)).

### A suggestion waits for yes, and then it stops

Shortwave’s praised assistant recommends bulk archive, label, delete, and todo actions, and the person confirms. The complaint, on the same product, is an assistant that archives what you wanted trashed. Morgen may move tasks while it is building a plan. After the user accepts, it stops, and a conflict gets a one-click reschedule. Attio’s MCP test asked approval before each write, and Ask Attio refused to guess a missing field. Canary Mail is the contrasting complaint: “summarize with AI” that cannot be fully turned off. Motion’s help page lists “tasks keep moving” as expected behavior ([Shortwave method](https://www.shortwave.com/docs/guides/method/); [WorkifyLab on Morgen](https://workifylab.com/best-calendar-apps-2026/); [Motion help](https://www.usemotion.com/help/time-management/auto-scheduling/reference-auto-scheduling/how-auto-scheduling-works-behind-the-scenes); [Canary](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/canary-mail-ai-email/id1155470386?platform=watch&see-all=reviews)).

### The sentence you are in is the only one at full strength

iA Writer’s Focus Mode highlights the current sentence or paragraph and fades the rest. Typora blurs other lines and renders the mark you just typed. Bear’s praised path is open a note and type. Ulysses can put the sidebar away. HEY’s long-term review describes one column. Claude’s write-ups praise a wide reading column, one accent, and no gradients or glows on the conversation. Obsidian and Notion are the other story: the praise starts after links or databases exist, and G2 assigns 23% of Notion’s concern mentions to learning curve and setup ([ia.net/writer](https://ia.net/writer); [G2](https://learn.g2.com/is-notion-worth-it); [FakeAIChat](https://www.fakeaichat.com/blog/ai-chat-ui-comparison-2026.html)).

### Color means a state, or it means whose class it is

Stripe teardowns limit color to succeeded, refunded, and failed. Mercury teardowns limit red and yellow to errors and warnings. Google Maps uses a fixed map language and three traffic colors. Oura’s redesign claims a semantic color so a score does not need a new legend, and the CHI paper shows the limit of that claim: people still could not tell which contributor to change. Course color in Kairos is already specified as data, a tag and a tick, and the brand highlighter is one yellow ([AdminLTE](https://adminlte.io/blog/fintech-dashboard-design-examples/); [Oura support](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/360025589793-Readiness-Score); [MASTER](../../design-system/kairos/MASTER.md)).

### Capture works from a small panel

iOS 26 Reminders takes a task from Control Center without opening the app. Todoist’s global Quick Add works while another app is focused, on desktop. Fantastical’s praised window is the menu bar, not the full calendar. Things’ Magic Plus can be dropped on an Inbox target without leaving the current list. Raycast is the extreme version: the launcher is the product. Akiflow’s diary says the phone parses a sentence and then requires an extra confirmation step, so the phone is for capture, not for planning ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/10/28/ios-26-gives-apples-reminders-app-a-new-faster-way-to-add-tasks/); [Todoist Quick Add](https://www.todoist.com/help/todoist/features/use-task-quick-add-in-todoist-va4Lhpzz)).

### The next step is a place in a sequence

Duolingo removed the skill grid because learners did not know the “correct” way. The path interleaves review so practice is forward motion, and a floating arrow returns you to the active node. Headspace’s course is a vertical sequence with a progress bar and a checkmark. Kairos’s own Study recipe already says progress is plain text, “3 of 6,” and MASTER lists progress bars with tracks, used as decoration, as an instant fail. The path and the bar are the evidence. The plain sentence is the version that already fits ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design/); [Pratt on Headspace](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2026/02/design-critique-headspace-ios-app-2/); [MASTER](../../design-system/kairos/MASTER.md)).

### A streak you can lose, and a push that notices you were gone

Duolingo states the mechanism. Early streak growth feels like a gain. Later growth is protected by loss aversion. A broken streak demotivates, and a freeze exists so a missed day does not end the habit. The company ties a richer post-lesson animation to a 1.7% lift in day-7 activity, a 2022 figure. Outside writers attack the counter and the owl, not the path order. A 2023 Journal of Consumer Research finding, as summarized by Lex on Tech rather than from the paper itself, says an intact streak increased the chance people continued, and that the effect tracked how the log was drawn. Headspace’s student critique describes the same shift, from the session to the defense of a number, and does not document a freeze. TickTick’s habits include streaks. Apple Reminders is praised in a 2026 review partly because it has no karma and no statistics. Oura’s crown is a threshold on today’s score, not a chain ([Duolingo, 31 January 2022](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit/); [DontSnooze](https://dontsnooze.io/blog/duolingo-guilt-notification-teardown/); [Calmevo](https://calmevo.com/apple-reminders-review/)).

### A feed starts playing and sits in front of the job

Spotify’s 2023 home is a vertical discovery surface with more autoplay, and a 2025 review still calls it a barrier to the user’s own playlists. YouTube Shorts complaints describe search, Home, and Subscriptions filled with a format that loops until you scroll, with no account-level off switch. Venmo’s distinctive craft is the feed, and it is also the privacy complaint. The 2026 redesign keeps the feed and, in a secondary account, adds offers to it. Headspace Explore is overlapping carousels of equal weight, which is the screen the course sequence was designed not to be. Cash App’s reputation in these sources is the absent feed ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2023/3/8/23630821/spotify-design-home-music-podcasts-audiobooks-app); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/youtube-shorts-new-features-2-3681602/); [Jotform](https://www.jotform.com/blog/venmo-vs-cash-app/)).

### The calendar keeps moving tasks after you look away

Motion will not place a task that lacks a duration and a deadline or a priority. Busy events win, and lower tasks move again when a meeting appears. Reviewers praise the placement and call the chrome cluttered, the phone desktop-first, and the billing the leading Trustpilot complaint. Reclaim books blocks from another tool into Google Calendar or Outlook, so the screen you stare at is still the calendar you already had, and the OAuth scope is every calendar you can access. Morgen is the control difference: it stops when the plan is accepted. Collapsing the three into “AI calendar” hides that difference ([Motion](https://www.usemotion.com/help/time-management/auto-scheduling); [G2, Reclaim](https://www.g2.com/compare/fantastical-vs-reclaim-ai)).

### Two people can see the draft, and the thread stays a thread

Front shows, in real time, that a teammate is replying, and the draft content updates immediately. Missive turns on collaborative cursors when a second person edits a reply. Capterra quotes from 9 October 2026 praise that shared draft. Both products add assignment and private comments onto one copy instead of turning the mail into a ticket first. The limit is structural: Missive’s collaborative drafts never appear in Gmail or Outlook, and Front’s review caveats are per-seat price and AI add-ons. This is the interaction reviewers praise, and it is a job for people answering someone else ([Front](https://help.front.com/en/articles/2403); [Missive](https://missiveapp.com/docs/core-features/conversations/drafts)).

### The write lands on this machine before it lands on a server

Obsidian’s vault is a folder of Markdown. The app is free, telemetry is disclaimed, and leaving is copying the folder. iA Writer and Typora are files. Bear is iCloud, and the vendor says it cannot read the notes. Linear’s August 2026 note describes a local database so that creating an issue does not wait on a round trip, with the server still the authority for the log. Notion, Craft, Ulysses, Heptabase, and Capacities keep a vendor library, and the dislike attached to that choice is lock-in: Markdown XL, limited offline, or a Sync plan you pay for in order to leave easily. Apple Notes is the loved default that fails retrieval once the archive is large, because the notes are not linked ([Obsidian pricing](https://obsidian.md/pricing); [Linear](https://linear.app/now/rebuilding-delta-sync-read-path)).

## Six to adopt, five to adapt, and four to leave

Kairos already specifies the page these scores assume. Home is a date, a greeting, one focus block, a few rows, and a semester line. Study is one question, prose at a reading size, and progress as plain text. Lists are hairline rows. Exactly one highlighter marks the single most important thing. Course color is a tag and a tick. There is no mascot, and voice presence has no losable state and no guilt ([MASTER](../../design-system/kairos/MASTER.md)). Adopt means the mechanic fits that page. Adapt means the idea is good and the usual SaaS execution conflicts. Leave means the mechanic conflicts with the product or with section 13.

### The day opens on one thing: Adopt

A Notebook version keeps the focus block for the single most important item and a hairline list under it. It refuses a dashboard of equal tiles, a 3-up card grid, a second highlighted row, and a score ring standing in for the item. Mercury’s balance and Maps’ place card are the same idea in another costume. The costume to refuse is the fintech chart grid and the map chrome. The page already has a place for this: the focus block is allowed once per screen, on a soft highlighter sheet, with one sentence and one primary button.

### What does not fit gets deferred on purpose: Adopt

A weekly or daily plan can sum the work against the hours a student actually has, and offer defer as a plain action on the row. Sunsama’s D and Z are the gesture. The Notebook version writes the consequence in a sentence (“This week is full. Move the reading to Thursday.”) and keeps today’s row on the soft highlighter. It refuses a shutdown ritual that posts to Slack, a share-to-Teams highlight reel, weekly stats as a score, and a focus timer that mutes other apps. Structured’s timeline can inform the calendar grid Kairos already allows, as hairline cells with course tags. It should not become a stack of overlapping cards.

### A suggestion waits for yes, and then it stops: Adopt

Kairos already previews anything that would be visible to someone else and does not execute it. The same rule belongs on the student’s own plan. A proposed order for the week, or a suggested calendar block, sits in a sheet until the student accepts it. After that, it does not rearrange itself because a new item arrived. Morgen is the model for the stop. Shortwave is the model for the confirm. Motion is the behavior to refuse. Canary’s always-on summarize control is the other refusal: an assistant affordance stuck to every open item, with no off switch. The sheet is a Notebook sheet, a page with a hairline, no backdrop blur.

### The sentence you are in is the only one at full strength: Adopt

Study is already this screen. One question, a reading column, no illustration over the prose, no margin note on top of the words. iA Writer’s fade is the optional emphasis, done with type weight and the single highlighter on the phrase that matters, not with a second theme, a proprietary markup, or a font menu. Claude’s wide column and single accent are the same idea. The serif-for-the-assistant and the terracotta are a different type system. Gabarito stays the face. The duplicate voice buttons in the Claude critique are a warning: if voice is offered, one control should say what it does, and the ink ring stays inside the voice sheet.

### Color means a state, or it means whose class it is: Adopt

Course color stays on the tag and on the semester-line tick. The highlighter marks one thing. Danger and success stay the only extra semantic colors, used when something failed or finished, the way Stripe uses color for a payment state. Oura’s five scores and crown are the version to refuse. A student does not need a 0–100 readiness number whose contributors they cannot act on. Maps’ traffic colors are a legend for a map Kairos does not have.

### The write lands on this machine before it lands on a server: Adopt

The inbox, the week, and the study notes already live under the student’s own files. The mechanic to keep is Obsidian’s and iA Writer’s: the artifact is on the machine, and a sync product is optional. Linear’s local write is the interaction version of the same idea, for a list that should not freeze while a request is in flight. The refusal is the blank vault, the plugin marketplace as the onboarding, the graph as the home screen, and Ulysses’ proprietary library. A first week should show the courses Canvas already returned, or an honest empty sentence. It should not show sample companies, sample courses, or a template gallery that implies the real week is a setup project.

### A typed line shows its date before it saves: Adapt

The idea belongs on the week list: one field, a sentence, a date that resolves into plain words (“Thu, Oct 15 at 11:59 PM”) before the row exists. Todoist’s highlight-as-you-type and Fantastical’s field split are the mechanic. The usual costume is a global launcher, a menu-bar mini window, or a glass command overlay. Those conflict with a page. The Notebook version is the field at the top of the list, ink on a sheet, the parsed date in muted type under the line, and the highlighter on that field only while it is the subject of the screen. It refuses a second chrome, a dark launcher, and a parse that files the item into a project the student did not see.

### The shortcut sits beside the action that runs it: Adapt

A local app with a week list, a course list, and a study session can use a sheet of actions. Superhuman and Linear show why: the palette teaches the key, and the key acts on the row you have selected. Vercel’s spec is the part to refuse. It asks for Title Case verb phrases (“Deploy Project”), and Kairos requires sentence case. Glass, blur, and a palette that becomes the product are out. Raycast replacing Spotlight is the wrong scope for a student app. The sheet opens on recent places in the week, traps focus, and closes back to the list. J and K can move down hairline rows. The first two days should not be slower than the mouse, which is the Superhuman complaint to design against.

### Incoming work sits in piles a person can correct: Adapt

Canvas already arrives as different kinds of items: work that is due, announcements, and grades. The mechanic worth taking is a small number of piles the student can correct, with a count of items still to handle rather than an unread badge. HEY’s three piles and Superhuman’s split counts are the evidence. The refusal is a second inbox that fills up and becomes the new unread pile, which is what long-term HEY users say the Screener and the Feed do. It is also an automatic classifier that hides a deadline in a tab and offers no per-sender correction. Spark’s Inbox Zero celebration, including a prompt to post about it, is the anti-pattern next door: a losable performance of emptiness. Three piles, named in sentences, as views of one list, fit the page. Seven splits, a command-palette library, and a celebration do not.

### Capture works from a small panel: Adapt

The Chrome side panel is already the “without opening the app” surface, at a narrow width, with the same row grammar. A compact capture into the local week belongs there: title, course, date, and save. Reminders’ Control Center panel is the precedent. Liquid Glass, a menu-bar product, and a system-wide hotkey that replaces Spotlight do not belong. Akiflow’s extra confirmation step on the phone is a useful constraint: the panel captures, and the plan still happens on the page, where the student can see what does not fit.

### The next step is a place in a sequence: Adapt

Study and exam prep already have a sequence: one question, then the next, with “3 of 6” in plain text, and today’s exam-prep row on the soft highlighter. Duolingo’s path is the evidence that a single next node beats a grid of skills. The Notebook version keeps the next question as the largest thing on the Study screen and writes the position as a sentence. It refuses the circle path, the floating game arrow, crowns, Legendary units, gem counters, and a progress bar whose track is decoration. Section 13 already treats that bar as an instant fail. Headspace’s large session cards, stacked as a card list, are the same idea in a costume of equal-weight cards. Rows, not cards.

### A streak you can lose, and a push that notices you were gone: Leave

This conflicts with the product and with the voice rules. MASTER says voice has no losable state and no guilt. The product already forbids a learning streak. Duolingo’s own post is the clearest sourced description of the mechanic: loss aversion, a demotivating break, and a freeze so the count survives. The sad-owl teardown says the guilt exists inside the notification. Headspace’s critique says the count changes why the session exists. TickTick’s habit streaks are the same object in a planner. A Notebook version would keep a checked row and a plain “3 of 6.” It would refuse the streak, the freeze, the repair, the owl, the push that performs disappointment, and any energy bar that subscribers do not have. There is no gentler execution that still is this mechanic. The path, scored above, is the part of Duolingo that survives. MASTER’s “warm like Duolingo” is that obvious next step and the plain-language unit title. It is not the character. There is no mascot.

### A feed starts playing and sits in front of the job: Leave

A weekly planner’s job is the week. Spotify Home, YouTube Shorts, Venmo’s payment feed, and Headspace Explore are all a catalog that starts itself and puts the library, the video someone searched for, or the send button behind it. Section 13 already forbids equal card grids. An infinite, autoplaying column is the same failure with motion. Kairos has no second surface whose job is to catch attention. Recommendations, if they exist, are the next row in the list the student already opened. They are not a home that plays.

### The calendar keeps moving tasks after you look away: Leave

A student calendar that rearranges classes and readings after the plan was accepted is the Motion complaint in a worse setting: the block that moved might be a class. Structured’s own App Store review asks for a lock so a class does not move. Reclaim’s wide write access to every calendar is the wrong scope for a local app. The acceptable piece of this family is already scored under “a suggestion waits for yes”: propose blocks, stop when they are accepted, and let the student move a row. The solver that owns the list, meters an AI credit, and calls movement expected behavior does not have a Notebook version. The cluttered desktop, and the phone app the company calls desktop-first, are the interface evidence, not only the policy.

### Two people can see the draft, and the thread stays a thread: Leave

Front and Missive are admired for a real problem Kairos does not have. The product does not submit, comment, or discussion-post, and it does not answer an instructor or a classmate on the student’s behalf. Assignment, collision detection, @mentions, and a live outbound draft are how a team inbox keeps two people from double-replying. Building them would invent a Canvas write. There is no Notebook restyling that makes that job in-bounds. The piece worth remembering, and it is already in the confirm rule, is that a draft stays a draft until a person sends it. The student sends it, in Canvas, themselves.

## The strongest fits are a page, a deferral, and a study column

The strongest fits for a weekly planner and a study session are the six Adopt mechanics plus typed capture: one item at the top of the page, a pass that admits the week is full, a suggestion that stops after yes, a study column, course color used as data, local files, and a date the student can see before the row exists. Things, Todoist, Sunsama, Structured, iA Writer, Bear, Apple Reminders, and Morgen’s stop-after-accept are the products those gestures come from. They are admired for refusing a second system. The research change is that “warm like Duolingo, calm like Things” splits in half under the sources. Things’ calm is the whole product. Duolingo’s warmth, in the company’s own writing, includes a count protected by loss aversion. Copy the path’s single next step. Leave the streak.

The wrong models are the products people keep paying for and keep describing as heavy. Jira’s 7,964 G2 reviews and 4.3 average sit next to a written complaint about fields, clicks, and noise, and Linear’s own fans already fear that shape. Salesforce’s related lists, setup search, and lost place in a list view are a CRM problem, not a week. ClickUp’s every-view density and Asana’s heavier controls are the team-suite version of the same mistake. Motion’s moving calendar, Duolingo’s streak and owl, and the homes of Spotify, YouTube Shorts, and Venmo are admired or enormous products whose second surface is the part reviewers resent. A student week is a page with one highlighter. It is not a queue, a feed, or a score you can lose.
